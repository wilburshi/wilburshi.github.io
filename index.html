Postdoctoral Fellow  ·  Yale University

Weikang Shi

I study how the primate brain computes strategic social decisions, with a focus on prosocial behavior in freely moving animals. My work integrates computational neuroethology, naturalistic behavioral paradigms, and large-scale neural recordings to link social strategies to their underlying neural computations.

My long-term goal is to build a circuit-level model of how the cortico-striatal circuit generates complex prosocial decisions.

I work with Drs. Steve Chang, Anirvan Nandy, and Monika Jadi at Yale's Wu Tsai Institute, supported by an NIH K99 Pathway to Independence Award.

Research

Social behavior requires integrating information about others' actions, intentions, and costs to make moment-to-moment decisions. My lab-in-training addresses this at three levels:

Behavior

Naturalistic cooperative paradigms in freely moving animal dyads, quantifying gaze, effort, and strategy in real time.

Computation

Drift-diffusion and game-theoretic models to formalize how social evidence is accumulated and converted into action.

Circuit

Large-scale recordings across prefrontal and striatal areas during naturalistic social interactions to identify the neural substrates of prosocial choice.

Long-term vision

A mechanistic, circuit-level model of altruistic decisions — with translational relevance to disorders of social cognition.
